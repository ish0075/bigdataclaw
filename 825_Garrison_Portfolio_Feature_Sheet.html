Key Validations

Revenue Assumptions

$2,000/unit/month rent is achievable in Niagara Falls/St. Catharines for newer buildings (e.g., near downtown, transit, or amenities). CMHC's Q1 2026 report shows Niagara's average rent for purpose-built 1-bed apartments at $1,950—so $2,000 is slightly above average but defensible for quality finishes or location premiums.

Parking/locker revenue adds ~2% to gross income—standard for urban/suburban infill projects.

Expense Ratio

Operating expenses = $383,000 ÷ $2,452,800 = 15.6% of gross income.

This is lower than typical (Niagara usually runs 25–35% for newer buildings due to taxes/maintenance), but plausible given:

  • Tenants pay their own utilities (saves ~$1,200/unit/year vs. all-inclusive).
  • Newer construction (lower maintenance/renovation needs).
  • Efficient property management (self-managed or tech-enabled).

Stress test: If expenses rose to 25% ($613,200), NOI would drop to $1,839,600 → net cash flow to $788,900 (still healthy 2.85% yield on cost).

Cost Structure & Debt Service

Total development cost: $27,650,000 ÷ 100 units = $276,500/unit.

  • Land: $30,000/unit (reasonable for Niagara infill).
  • Hard costs: $200,000/unit (at the low end but feasible for wood-frame/mid-rise in Ontario 2026—especially with standardized designs or modular elements).
  • Soft costs (dev charges, financing, servicing, etc.): $46,500/unit (typical for Niagara municipalities).

CMHC loan: 3.8% interest-only on $27.65M = $1,050,700/year.

Loan-to-Cost (LTC): $27.65M ÷ $27.65M = 100% (unusual—CMHC usually caps at 85–90% LTC for rental projects).

Note: The user states "Mortgage is interest only 50 year amortization"—this likely means interest-only payments with principal due at maturity (balloon), not true 50-year amortization (which CMHC doesn't offer). If it's a standard 25-year amortization at 3.8%, annual debt service would be ~$1.68M (not $1.05M), turning cash flow negative. Clarification needed here.

Net Cash Flow Yield

$1,019,100 ÷ $27,650,000 = 3.69% unlevered yield on cost.

After debt service: 3.69% levered cash-on-cash (assuming 100% CMHC financing—highly aggressive; if LTC is 80%, equity = $5.53M → cash-on-cash = 18.4%).

For context: Niagara's current cap rates for stabilized rental are 4.5–5.5%, so 3.69% yield on cost implies modest value-add potential (rent growth or expense compression needed to hit market cap rates).
